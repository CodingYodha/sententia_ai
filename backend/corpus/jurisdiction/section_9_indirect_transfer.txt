INCOME TAX ACT 1961 — SECTION 9(1)(i): INDIRECT TRANSFER OF INDIAN ASSETS
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India
Amended by: Finance Act 2012 (retrospective amendment), Finance Act 2015, Finance Act 2017

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. STATUTORY TEXT — SECTION 9(1)(i)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Section 9(1) of the Income Tax Act 1961 provides for the following income to be deemed
to accrue or arise in India:

9(1)(i): All income accruing or arising, whether directly or indirectly, through or from
any business connection in India, or through or from any property in India, or through
or from any asset or source of income in India, or through the transfer of a capital asset
situate in India.

EXPLANATION 5 (inserted by Finance Act 2012):
For the purposes of this clause, an asset or a capital asset being any share or interest
in a company or entity registered or incorporated outside India shall be deemed to be
situated in India, if the share or interest derives, directly or indirectly, its value
substantially from the assets located in India.

EXPLANATION 6 (amended by Finance Act 2015):
"Substantially" for purposes of Explanation 5 means more than 50% of the fair market
value of the total assets of the company or entity is attributable to assets located in India.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
2. THE 50% VALUE DERIVATION TEST
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A share/interest in a foreign company (e.g., Singapore SPV, Cayman holdco) is deemed
to be a capital asset "situated in India" if:

TEST: Value derived from Indian assets > 50% of total assets of the foreign entity

CALCULATION METHOD (Finance Act 2015):
- Numerator: Fair market value of Indian assets held (directly or indirectly) by
  the foreign entity
- Denominator: Fair market value of ALL assets held (directly or indirectly) by
  the foreign entity
- Ratio > 50% → share/interest deemed situated in India → capital gains from transfer
  taxable in India in the hands of non-resident transferor

EXAMPLE:
  Singapore HoldCo assets:
  - 74% stake in Indian OpCo: FMV USD 100M (constitutes 80% of total assets)
  - Cash in Singapore: USD 25M (20% of total assets)
  
  India-derived value: 80% > 50% threshold → Section 9(1)(i) applies
  Transfer of shares in Singapore HoldCo by non-resident → Capital gains taxable in India

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
3. SMALL SHAREHOLDER EXEMPTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Finance Act 2015 introduced an exemption for small shareholders:

EXEMPTION APPLIES IF BOTH CONDITIONS MET:
(a) The transferor does not hold (directly or indirectly) more than 5% of the share
    capital or voting power of the foreign company/entity at any time during the
    12 months preceding the date of transfer; AND
(b) The gains from transfer do not arise to a company, firm, or other association of
    persons in which Indian residents hold more than 50% interest

This exemption protects genuine minority portfolio investors from inadvertent India
tax exposure on transfers of foreign company shares.

4. TREATY OVERRIDE — DTAA PROTECTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Section 90(2) of the Income Tax Act provides that DTAA provisions shall prevail to the
extent they are more beneficial to the taxpayer. However:

SINGAPORE-INDIA DTAA — CAPITAL GAINS (Article 13):
- Pre-April 1, 2017 investments: Capital gains exempt from Indian tax (grandfathered)
- Post-April 1, 2017 investments: Capital gains taxable in India on source basis
  The 2016 Protocol (effective 2017) substantially eliminated the capital gains
  exemption for Singapore-routed investments in India

MAURITIUS-INDIA DTAA — CAPITAL GAINS (Article 13):
- Similar grandfathering: Pre-April 1, 2017 investments: exempt
- Post-April 1, 2017: India has right to tax capital gains
- "Limitation of benefits" clause applies — shell companies without substance
  in Mauritius may not qualify

NETHERLANDS-INDIA DTAA:
- Contains robust capital gains article; remains relevant for PE/VC structures

PRINCIPAL PURPOSE TEST (PPT — Post-BEPS):
Following India's adoption of BEPS Multilateral Instrument, most Indian DTAAs now
include the PPT clause. DTAA benefits can be denied if it is reasonable to conclude
that obtaining treaty benefit was one of the principal purposes of an arrangement.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
5. WITHHOLDING OBLIGATION — SECTION 195
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Any person responsible for paying to a non-resident any sum chargeable under the
Income Tax Act must deduct tax at source (TDS/withholding) under Section 195.

For indirect transfer transactions:
- The Indian entity (if it has knowledge of the transfer) may have a withholding obligation
- The acquirer in an indirect transfer may need to withhold if the Indian assets are
  acquired through the foreign structure
- Application for lower/nil deduction certificate (Section 197) available from
  jurisdictional assessing officer

REPORTING — RULE 114DB:
Non-residents transferring shares/interest in a foreign entity where Section 9(1)(i)
Explanation 5 applies must file Form 3CT in India within 90 days of transfer.
Failure to report is a separate compliance default.

6. PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS FOR SPV STRUCTURING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SCENARIO 1 — SPV predominantly holding Indian assets:
Chinese HoldCo holds Singapore SPV. Singapore SPV holds 74% of Indian OpCo (FMV USD 100M)
and no other significant assets. If Chinese HoldCo transfers Singapore SPV shares to a
third party, Section 9(1)(i) Explanation 5 applies: Indian assets constitute >50% of
SPV value. Capital gains are deemed to arise in India and are taxable in India.

MITIGATION STRATEGIES:
1. Diversify SPV assets (hold non-Indian assets to bring India % below 50%) — complex
2. Hold for long-term and rely on re-negotiated DTAA protection — uncertain post-2017
3. Restructure before exit to direct India holding — may trigger other tax events
4. Apply for advance ruling from Authority for Advance Rulings (AAR) — time-consuming

SCENARIO 2 — SPV with diversified global assets:
If the Singapore SPV holds multiple country investments and Indian assets constitute
only 30% of total SPV value → Section 9(1)(i) Explanation 5 does NOT apply. Transfer
of SPV shares is not deemed as Indian asset transfer. No India tax on transfer.

KEY PLANNING POINT:
The 50% test is calculated at the time of transfer. Asset values fluctuate. Pre-exit
asset valuation analysis is critical to determine whether Section 9(1)(i) applies
and to assess treaty protection availability.
