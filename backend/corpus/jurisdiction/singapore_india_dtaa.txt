SINGAPORE-INDIA DOUBLE TAXATION AVOIDANCE AGREEMENT (DTAA)
Official Title: Agreement Between the Government of the Republic of Singapore and the
Government of the Republic of India for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the
Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with Respect to Taxes on Income
First Signed: 1981 | Substantially Amended: 2005 | Protocol of 2016 (effective 2017)
Source: Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore (IRAS); CBDT India

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. OVERVIEW AND STRATEGIC IMPORTANCE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Singapore-India DTAA is one of India's most commercially significant bilateral tax
treaties, governing the tax treatment of investment flows between two of Asia's largest
financial and investment hubs. Singapore is the largest source of FDI into India from
a treaty jurisdiction.

The 2016 Protocol fundamentally changed the capital gains treatment under this DTAA,
eliminating the capital gains exemption for investments made after April 1, 2017 and
introducing a Principal Purpose Test (PPT) as an anti-avoidance measure.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
2. CAPITAL GAINS — ARTICLE 13 (POST-2016 PROTOCOL)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PRE-APRIL 1, 2017 INVESTMENTS (GRANDFATHERED):
Capital gains on shares acquired before April 1, 2017 remain covered by the original
Article 13, which provided that gains from alienation of shares in an Indian company
by a Singapore resident were taxable only in Singapore (where India's capital gains
tax was effectively exempt). This grandfathering is permanently preserved for such shares.

POST-MARCH 31, 2017 INVESTMENTS (NEW REGIME):
Capital gains from alienation of shares in an Indian company are taxable in India.
Specifically:
- Source Rule: India has the right to tax capital gains on Indian company shares
- Rate: As per Indian domestic tax law (for long-term capital gains: 10% for listed,
  10%/20% for unlisted depending on holding period and indexation)
- Transition Period (FY 2017-18 and FY 2018-19): 50% tax exemption (phased in)
- From FY 2019-20 onwards: Full India taxation applies

INDIRECT TRANSFER INTERACTION:
Even where Singapore-India DTAA Article 13 provides capital gains rights to India,
the indirect transfer provisions of Section 9(1)(i) of India's Income Tax Act may
apply to transfers of Singapore company shares (not direct Indian shares). Post-2017,
DTAA protection for indirect transfers is significantly reduced.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
3. DIVIDENDS — ARTICLE 10
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Withholding tax on dividends paid by Indian company to Singapore shareholder:
- If Singapore shareholder holds ≥ 25% of Indian company shares: 10% withholding
- Other cases: 15% withholding
- Under Indian domestic law: Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) was abolished from
  FY 2020-21; dividends now taxable in shareholder's hands at treaty rates
  
BENEFIT: Lower treaty rate (10%/15%) vs Indian domestic withholding rate.
SUBSTANCE REQUIREMENT: Singapore recipient must be the beneficial owner of dividends.
A Singapore SPV that immediately pass-throughs dividends to a third-country investor
without real business activity may not qualify as beneficial owner (see Article 10(2)).

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
4. INTEREST — ARTICLE 11
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Withholding tax on interest paid from India to Singapore lender:
- General rate: 15%
- For banking institutions and financial enterprises: 10%

BENEFIT vs DOMESTIC LAW: Indian domestic withholding on interest is 40% for corporate
non-residents (or 20% under Section 115A). Treaty rate is significantly more favorable.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
5. ROYALTIES AND FEES FOR TECHNICAL SERVICES — ARTICLE 12
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Withholding tax on royalties and FTS paid from India to Singapore:
- Royalties: 10% (reduced from 15% by 2005 protocol)
- Fees for Technical Services: 10%
- Domestic rate without treaty: 10%-20%

For IP-holding structures (Singapore holding IP, licensing to Indian entity):
- Royalty withholding at 10% is favorable
- Principal Purpose Test (PPT — introduced by 2016 Protocol) may deny benefit if
  principal purpose of IP transfer to Singapore was to obtain reduced withholding

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
6. ANTI-AVOIDANCE — PRINCIPAL PURPOSE TEST (PPT)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

2016 Protocol introduced Article 24A — Limitation of Benefits:

A benefit under this Convention shall not be granted in respect of an item of income
if it is reasonable to conclude, having regard to all relevant facts and circumstances,
that obtaining that benefit was one of the principal purposes of any arrangement or
transaction that resulted directly or indirectly in that benefit, unless it is established
that granting that benefit in these circumstances would be in accordance with the object
and purpose of the relevant provisions of this Convention.

PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS:
- Shell companies in Singapore without economic substance → at risk of PPT denial
- Singapore SPVs set up solely to access DTAA benefits without real business → at risk
- SAFE HARBOR: Companies with genuine economic substance in Singapore (offices, employees,
  management and control in Singapore, meaningful business operations) → lower PPT risk
- Singapore Economic Development Board (EDB) certification of substantive business → helpful

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
7. RESIDENCE — ARTICLE 4
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Tax residence for treaty purposes is determined by domestic law of each country.
For companies:
- Singapore: Incorporated or managed and controlled in Singapore
- India: Incorporated in India (domicile test)

DUAL RESIDENCE TIE-BREAKER:
For companies resident in both countries, place of effective management (POEM) is the
tie-breaker. India's POEM rules (effective FY 2017-18) treat a foreign company as Indian
tax resident if its POEM is in India (i.e., key management and commercial decisions are
made in India).

IMPACT ON SPV STRUCTURING:
A Singapore SPV whose directors routinely take instructions from Indian parent or make
decisions while physically in India risks being treated as India-tax-resident (POEM in
India), negating all DTAA benefits and exposing the Singapore entity to full Indian taxation.
SUBSTANCE IN SINGAPORE IS CRITICAL: Board meetings must genuinely occur in Singapore,
directors must exercise independent judgment in Singapore, management must not be delegated
to India-based officers.

8. COMPREHENSIVE ECONOMIC COOPERATION AGREEMENT (CECA) — INVESTMENT PROVISIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Singapore-India CECA (2005) supplements the DTAA with investment protection provisions:

NATIONAL TREATMENT: Each Party shall accord to investors of the other Party and their
investments, treatment no less favorable than it accords to its own investors in like
circumstances with respect to management, use, and enjoyment of investments.

MFN TREATMENT: Investors of each Party shall receive treatment no less favorable than
that accorded to investors of any third state.

INVESTOR-STATE DISPUTE SETTLEMENT (ISDS):
Disputes between an investor of one Party and the other Party relating to an investment
may be submitted to international arbitration (ICSID or UNCITRAL Rules).

EXPROPRIATION PROTECTION:
Neither Party shall nationalize or expropriate investments of investors of the other
Party directly or indirectly except: (a) for public purpose; (b) on a non-discriminatory
basis; (c) in accordance with due process; and (d) accompanied by prompt and adequate
compensation at fair market value.

PRACTICAL USE:
CECA investment chapter provides Singapore investors in India a layer of protection
beyond the DTAA — applicable to all investments, regardless of when made.
The ISDS mechanism provides an alternative to Indian courts for dispute resolution.
