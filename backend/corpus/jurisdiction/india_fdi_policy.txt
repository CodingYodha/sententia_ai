INDIA FDI POLICY — CONSOLIDATED SUMMARY
DPIIT Consolidated FDI Policy 2020 (as amended through 2026)
Source: Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Government of India

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. OVERVIEW AND LEGAL FRAMEWORK
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

India's Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) policy is governed by the Foreign Exchange
Management Act 1999 (FEMA), the FEMA (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules 2019, and the
DPIIT Consolidated FDI Policy updated periodically. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
and DPIIT jointly administer FDI regulations in India.

FDI is permitted through two routes:

AUTOMATIC ROUTE: FDI does not require prior government approval. The foreign investor
and the Indian company receiving FDI only need to notify RBI within 30 days of receiving
funds (Form FC-GPR) and within 60 days of transfer of shares (Form FC-TRS). This route
covers the majority of sectors.

GOVERNMENT ROUTE (Prior Approval Route): FDI requires prior approval of the Government
through the Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal (FIFP) administered by DPIIT. The
competent authority varies by sector (e.g., Ministry of Defence for defence sector,
Ministry of Civil Aviation for airports). Government route cases are processed within
60 working days.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
2. PROHIBITED SECTORS (FDI NOT PERMITTED)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

FDI is prohibited in the following sectors:
- Lottery business (including government/private/online lottery)
- Gambling and betting (including casinos)
- Chit funds
- Nidhi company
- Trading in Transferable Development Rights (TDRs)
- Real estate business or construction of farm houses
- Manufacturing of cigars, cheroots, cigarillos, and cigarettes (tobacco products)
- Activities/sectors not open to private sector investment (atomic energy, railway operations)
- Agricultural or plantation activities (with exceptions for tea plantation, horticulture, etc.)

3. KEY SECTORS AND FDI CAPS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

AUTOMATIC ROUTE — 100% FDI:
- Information Technology (IT/ITES/BPO)
- Pharmaceuticals (greenfield)
- Chemicals
- Food processing
- Textiles and garments
- Construction development (townships, housing, built-up infrastructure) — subject to conditions
- E-commerce (marketplace model only; inventory-based FDI not permitted)
- Agri-tech and precision farming
- Renewable energy
- Infrastructure (roads, highways, ports)

AUTOMATIC ROUTE — SECTOR CAPS:
- Private sector banking: up to 74% automatic; beyond 74% requires government approval
- Insurance: 74% (automatic route)
- Telecom: 100% (automatic up to 49%; government route above 49%)
- Civil aviation (greenfield airports): 100% automatic; brownfield airports: 74% automatic, beyond requires government
- Pharmaceuticals (brownfield): 74% automatic; beyond 74% requires government approval

GOVERNMENT ROUTE:
- Defence: up to 74% automatic; beyond 74% requires government (with access to modern technology)
- Multi-brand retail trading: 51%, government route, subject to conditions (30% local sourcing)
- Banking (public sector): 20% cap, government route
- Print media: 26% cap, government route
- Broadcasting (terrestrial/DTH): various caps, government route

4. COMPLIANCE AND REPORTING OBLIGATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

FC-GPR (Foreign Currency-Gross Provisional Return): 
Filed by Indian company with RBI within 30 days of receiving FDI. Reports issue of
shares/convertible instruments. Filed online via FIRMS portal.

FC-TRS (Foreign Currency-Transfer of Shares):
Filed when shares are transferred between resident and non-resident parties. Filed
within 60 days of transfer.

FCTRS: Annual return filed by Indian company for every financial year for outstanding
foreign investments.

VALUATION REQUIREMENTS:
- Shares must be issued to foreign investor at price not less than fair market value
  determined by internationally accepted pricing methodology (DCF for unlisted companies;
  SEBI guidelines for listed)
- For secondary transfers, price must be within the pricing range determined by registered
  merchant banker

DOWNSTREAM INVESTMENT:
When an Indian company with foreign investment makes investment in another Indian entity,
it is treated as indirect foreign investment if foreign investor holds more than 50% in
the Indian investing company. Downstream investment follows the same sectoral caps and
conditions as direct FDI.

5. FDI FROM LAND-BORDER COUNTRIES — PRESS NOTE 3 (2020) AND PRESS NOTE 2 (2026)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

[See separate document: Press Note 3 (2020 Series) for full details]

Amendment introduced by Press Note 3 (2020) requires government approval for all FDI
from entities in countries sharing a land border with India. Affected countries include:
China, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Nepal, Myanmar (Burma), Bhutan, and Afghanistan.

Key implications:
- Applies to ALL sectors, even those otherwise on automatic route
- Applies to beneficial ownership — even if immediate investor is from a non-land-border
  country, if the ultimate beneficial owner is from a land-border country, government
  approval is required
- Applies to transfer of existing investments as well as new FDI
- No threshold — any amount of investment from land-border country entities requires approval

Press Note 2 (2026) further clarified the UBO tracing methodology and extended the scope
to cover indirect holdings through multi-layered structures.
