OECD MODEL TAX CONVENTION ON INCOME AND CAPITAL — 2019 CONDENSED VERSION
Key Articles and Commentary Relevant to Cross-Border Investment Structuring
Source: OECD Publishing, Paris. OECD (2019), Model Tax Convention on Income and Capital
Condensed Version — July 2010 (as updated through 2019 and 2024)

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
OVERVIEW AND SIGNIFICANCE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The OECD Model Tax Convention (MTC) is the foundational template upon which the vast
majority of bilateral tax treaties (DTAAs) worldwide are based. Over 3,000 bilateral
treaties follow the OECD MTC structure. Understanding the Model Convention and its
official Commentary is essential for interpreting bilateral treaty provisions.

India negotiates its treaties broadly following the OECD MTC but with UN Model Convention
influences (stronger source-country taxation rights). The OECD MTC Commentary (though not
legally binding) is routinely cited by Indian courts, tax tribunals, and the Authority for
Advance Rulings (AAR) in interpreting treaty provisions.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
ARTICLE 1 — PERSONS COVERED / BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Convention applies to persons who are residents of one or both of the Contracting States.

BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP (Article 10, 11, 12):
Treaty-reduced withholding rates are available only to the "beneficial owner" of income.
A conduit company — one that receives income but is obligated by contract or law to
pass it on to another person — is NOT the beneficial owner.

OECD Commentary Para 12.1 on Article 10:
"A conduit company cannot be regarded as the beneficial owner if, though the formal owner
of the income, it has, as a practical matter, very narrow powers which render it, in relation
to the income concerned, a mere fiduciary or administrator acting on account of the interested parties."

PRACTICAL TEST (derived from Commentary):
- Does the receiving entity have the right to use and enjoy the income?
- Is the receiving entity subject to a contractual or legal obligation to pass income on?
- Does the receiving entity bear any economic risk on the income?

A Singapore SPV that holds Indian company shares and receives dividends, then distributes
100% immediately to its parent with no discretion → risk of "conduit" characterization.
A Singapore entity with own employees, real operations, genuine equity risk, discretion
over distributions → more likely to be treated as beneficial owner.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
ARTICLE 4 — RESIDENT / PLACE OF EFFECTIVE MANAGEMENT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Residence is determined by domestic law. For companies that are dual residents, the
tie-breaker is the Place of Effective Management (POEM):

"The place of effective management is the place where the key management and commercial
decisions that are necessary for the conduct of the entity's business as a whole are
in substance made."

2017 Update Commentary: POEM determination requires examination of:
1. Where meetings of board of directors are held
2. Where the chief executive officer and senior management habitually reside
3. Where the senior day-to-day management is carried on
4. Where the entity's headquarters are located
5. Where the management's decisions are made

This is consistent with India's domestic POEM rules (Section 6(3)(ii) Income Tax Act
as amended by Finance Act 2016).

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
ARTICLE 5 — PERMANENT ESTABLISHMENT (PE)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A "permanent establishment" means a fixed place of business through which the business
of an enterprise is wholly or partly carried on. If a foreign enterprise has a PE in
the source country, the source country may tax business profits attributable to the PE.

TYPES OF PE:
Fixed place PE: Office, branch, factory, workshop, mine, oil/gas well
Agency PE: A person acting on behalf of an enterprise habitually exercising authority
  to conclude contracts in the source country (not merely as independent agent)
Construction PE: Building site or construction project that lasts more than 12 months
Service PE: (UN Model / some bilateral treaties) Personnel providing services in source
  country for more than 183 days in any 12-month period

BEPS ACTION 7 — ANTI-FRAGMENTATION (2016 Update):
Artificial avoidance of PE status through fragmented activities or artificial structures
(e.g., splitting contracts, using multiple entities) is addressed. Commissionnaire
structures and dependent agent PE rules were expanded.

RELEVANCE FOR SPV STRUCTURES:
If a Singapore SPV (holding Indian company shares) has employees or agents in India
making investment decisions, managing operations, or exercising authority → risk of
creating an Indian PE for the Singapore entity. PE in India → business profits (including
capital gains management income) may be taxable in India.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
ARTICLE 7 — BUSINESS PROFITS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Business profits of a resident of a Contracting State are taxable only in that State
unless the enterprise carries on business through a PE in the other State.

If PE exists: Profits attributable to the PE are taxable in the PE's jurisdiction.
Attribution follows OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines and the "authorized OECD approach"
(functionally separate entity principle).

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
ARTICLE 13 — CAPITAL GAINS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

OECD MTC DEFAULT POSITION:
Gains from alienation of shares deriving more than 50% of their value from immovable
property (real estate) situated in the source state → source state has taxing rights.
Other gains from shares → residence state has exclusive taxing rights.

COUNTRY MODIFICATIONS:
Many bilateral treaties modify the default MTC position. India has negotiated source-based
capital gains taxation in many of its treaties (particularly post-BEPS MLI ratification).

2017 ADDITION — SHARES RICH IN IMMOVABLE PROPERTY:
New paragraph added: Gains from alienation of shares deriving more than 50% of their
value from immovable property may be taxed in the state where the immovable property
is situated. This provision prevents avoidance of real estate capital gains taxes through
offshore holding companies.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
ARTICLE 26 — EXCHANGE OF INFORMATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Tax authorities shall exchange information that is foreseeably relevant for carrying out
the provisions of the Convention or domestic tax laws of the Contracting States.
Information may be used for tax purposes only and shall be kept confidential.

SIGNIFICANCE:
Post-CRS (Common Reporting Standard) and FATCA, the exchange of information between
tax authorities has become routine and automatic. Structures relying on opacity between
jurisdictions are no longer viable planning tools. UBO information disclosed in one
jurisdiction (e.g., Qdrant registry in Singapore) may be automatically reported to
Indian tax authorities under the multilateral instrument implementing CRS.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
MULTILATERAL INSTRUMENT (MLI) — BEPS CONVENTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The OECD/G20 Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Multilateral Instrument (MLI),
effective for India from April 1, 2020, modifies covered tax agreements (India's
bilateral treaties with 25+ countries including Singapore, Mauritius, Netherlands, UAE)
to include:

1. MINIMUM STANDARD ANTI-ABUSE (Article 6 MLI — PPT):
   Principal Purpose Test applies to all covered agreements. Treaty benefits denied
   if obtaining the benefit was one of the principal purposes of the arrangement.

2. PERMANENT ESTABLISHMENT — ANTI-FRAGMENTATION (Article 13-15 MLI):
   Prevents artificial PE avoidance through activity fragmentation.

3. DISPUTE RESOLUTION — MAP (Article 16 MLI):
   Mutual Agreement Procedure improvements for treaty interpretation disputes.

KEY PRACTICAL IMPLICATION:
The MLI means that the Principal Purpose Test NOW applies to Singapore-India,
Mauritius-India, Netherlands-India, UAE-India (and other covered treaties) DTAAs.
Treaty shopping is subject to active PPT challenge by tax authorities.
