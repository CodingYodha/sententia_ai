1. OVERVIEW — MAS AS INTEGRATED FINANCIAL REGULATOR
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Monetary Authority of Singapore is Singapore's central bank and integrated financial
regulator. MAS supervises all financial services entities in Singapore including:
- Banks and finance companies
- Insurance companies
- Capital markets intermediaries (brokers, dealers, advisers)
- Fund managers and collective investment schemes
- Payment service providers
- Exchange operators

MAS operates under a principles-based regulatory philosophy, emphasizing outcomes over
prescriptive rules, and provides comprehensive regulatory guidance through:
- Notices (legally binding)
- Guidelines (best practice, not legally binding but influential)
- Circulars (policy announcements)
- Consultation Papers (proposed regulatory changes)

2. CAPITAL MARKETS — LICENSING FRAMEWORK
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

SECURITIES AND FUTURES ACT 2001 (SFA):
Governs securities and derivatives markets in Singapore. Key licenses:

CAPITAL MARKETS SERVICES (CMS) LICENSE:
Required for conducting regulated activities including:
- Dealing in capital markets products (securities, units in collective investment schemes)
- Fund management
- Providing custodial services for securities
- Securities financing
- Providing credit rating services

LICENSE EXEMPTIONS:
Certain persons are exempt from CMS licensing requirements:
- Banks licensed under Banking Act
- Certain foreign companies regulated in their home jurisdiction (Section 99(1)(h) SFA)
- "Sophisticated" investors dealing between themselves (institutional investor exemptions)

FUND MANAGEMENT LICENSING:
Registered Fund Management Company (RFMC): For smaller fund managers managing ≤ S$250M
AUM with ≤ 30 qualified investors. Registration (not full licensing) required.
Licensed Fund Management Company (LFMC): Full CMS license required for larger managers.
  - Retail LFMC: May manage retail funds, stricter requirements
  - Accredited/Institutional LFMC: May only manage sophisticated investor funds

3. VARIABLE CAPITAL COMPANY (VCC) — FUND STRUCTURE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

OVERVIEW:
The Variable Capital Company Act (VCC Act) was enacted in 2018 (effective January 2020)
to create a new corporate structure specifically designed for investment funds in Singapore.
VCCs have rapidly gained adoption — over 1,000 VCCs registered since inception.

WHAT IS A VCC?
A VCC is a corporate entity specifically designed to serve as a fund vehicle with:
- Variable share capital (can issue/redeem shares without shareholder approval)
- Umbrella structure: One VCC can house multiple sub-funds (each with ring-fenced assets)
- Flexible dividend distribution from capital (unlike standard Singapore companies)
- Simplified regulatory requirements compared to collective investment scheme structure

ELIGIBLE FUND MANAGERS:
Must be managed by an MAS-licensed or registered fund manager (LFMC or RFMC).

VCC ADVANTAGES FOR CROSS-BORDER INVESTMENT STRUCTURES:
1. Singapore Income Tax Act provides tax exemptions for qualifying VCC funds (Section 13O, 13U)
2. GST remission schemes for VCC fund management expenses
3. Recognized as a fund structure in key jurisdictions (Australia, Luxembourg)
4. Sub-fund ring-fencing: Liability of one sub-fund does not cross-contaminate others
5. Can take on partnership-style economics while maintaining corporate limited liability

TAX TREATMENT OF VCC (Section 13O and 13U Schemes):
VCC sub-funds managed by approved fund managers may qualify for:
- Section 13O (formerly 13R): Singapore Resident Fund Scheme — tax exemption on
  specified income (dividends, interest, gains from disposal of investments)
  REQUIREMENTS: AUM ≥ S$50M, 2+ fund management employees in Singapore, local spend ≥ S$200K/year
- Section 13U (formerly 13X): Enhanced Tier Fund Scheme — broader asset class coverage
  REQUIREMENTS: AUM ≥ S$50M; minimum local expenditure (higher than 13O); 3+ investment
  professionals based in Singapore

RELEVANCE FOR CROSS-BORDER STRUCTURES:
A Gulf SWF investing into India through a Singapore VCC sub-fund can:
(a) Benefit from Singapore-India DTAA (if VCC qualifies as Singapore tax resident)
(b) Obtain MAS-regulated status for the investment vehicle
(c) Maintain ring-fenced liability at sub-fund level
(d) Benefit from Singapore's neutral tax treatment on fund income

4. AML/CFT — ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING FRAMEWORK
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

MAS NOTICE 626 (Banks) and equivalent notices for other financial institution types:
Comprehensive AML/CFT framework incorporating FATF (Financial Action Task Force) standards.

KEY REQUIREMENTS FOR INVESTMENT STRUCTURES:
CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD):
- Standard CDD: Verify identity of customer, beneficial owner (>25% ownership/control)
- Enhanced CDD: For higher-risk customers (PEPs, high-risk jurisdictions, complex structures)
- Ongoing monitoring: Transaction monitoring for unusual patterns

BENEFICIAL OWNER IDENTIFICATION:
For corporate customers: Identify natural persons owning ≥ 25% of shares/voting rights
(Singapore threshold consistent with FATF Recommendation 10). Singapore financial institutions
must look through multiple layers of corporate structure to identify natural person UBOs.

POLITICALLY EXPOSED PERSONS (PEPs):
Enhanced due diligence for PEPs, their family members, and close associates. Singapore
has a broad PEP definition aligned with FATF guidance. Government officials, state-owned
enterprise directors, and senior politicians from any jurisdiction.

HIGH-RISK JURISDICTIONS:
FATF blacklist and greylist jurisdictions require enhanced AML measures. Transactions
involving entities from high-risk jurisdictions face additional scrutiny from Singapore
financial institutions regardless of nominal investor domicile.

5. SINGAPORE AS INVESTMENT HOLDING HUB — ADVANTAGES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

POLITICAL AND LEGAL STABILITY:
Rule of law, independent judiciary, English legal system, common law heritage.
Consistent regulatory environment. Top-ranked for ease of doing business.

TREATY NETWORK:
Singapore has DTAAs with 100+ countries including India, China, UAE, US, Germany, Netherlands.
Singapore's treaty network is one of the most extensive in Asia.

PARTICIPATION EXEMPTION / DIVIDEND EXEMPTION:
Dividends received by Singapore holding company from foreign subsidiaries are generally
exempt from Singapore tax under Section 13(8) Income Tax Act (Singapore) if:
(a) Headline tax rate of foreign jurisdiction ≥ 15%; AND
(b) Dividends were subject to tax in foreign jurisdiction

CAPITAL GAINS:
Singapore has no capital gains tax. Gains from sale of investments are generally not
taxable in Singapore (unless classified as trading income — test based on frequency,
nature, purpose of investment).

NO WITHHOLDING TAX ON DIVIDENDS:
Singapore does not impose withholding tax on dividends paid to non-residents.
This makes Singapore a highly efficient dividend repatriation hub.

TRANSFER PRICING:
Singapore has transfer pricing rules aligned with OECD guidelines (Section 34D ITA Singapore).
Related party transactions must be at arm's length. Advance Pricing Arrangements (APAs)
available from IRAS.
