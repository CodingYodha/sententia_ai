EU FDI SCREENING REGULATION — REGULATION (EU) 2019/452
Framework for Screening of Foreign Direct Investments into the European Union
Source: European Commission; Official Journal of the EU L 79I, 21.3.2019
Effective: October 11, 2020 | Proposal for strengthened Regulation: 2024

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. OVERVIEW AND PURPOSE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Regulation (EU) 2019/452 established a Union framework for screening FDI on the grounds
of security or public order. The Regulation does NOT create a Union-level FDI approval
mechanism — it creates a COOPERATION FRAMEWORK between Member States and the Commission,
while leaving screening authority with individual Member States.

RATIONALE: To ensure that FDI that potentially threatens EU security or public order
undergoes proper screening, and to allow exchange of information and coordination between
Member States on transactions that may affect multiple Member States or EU-level interests.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
2. SCOPE — WHAT IS COVERED
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

COVERED INVESTMENT:
FDI into the EU: An investment of any kind by a foreign investor aiming to establish or
maintain lasting and direct links between the foreign investor and the undertaking to
which capital is made available (typically direct investments resulting in ≥ 10% shareholding).

NOT COVERED:
- Portfolio investments (below control threshold, no "lasting links")
- Intra-EU investments (investment from one EU Member State to another)
- Greenfield investments in new sectors (though Member States may screen independently)

SECTORS OF CONCERN (Article 4 — non-exhaustive list for Member States):
Member States and the Commission shall take the following into account when screening:
1. Critical infrastructure (energy, transport, water, health, communications, media,
   data processing, aerospace, defense, financial, electoral/voting infrastructure)
2. Critical technologies and dual-use items (AI, robotics, semiconductors, cybersecurity,
   aerospace, defense, energy storage, quantum, nuclear technologies)
3. Supply of critical inputs (energy, raw materials, food security)
4. Access to sensitive information (personal data, health data, IP)
5. Freedom and pluralism of the media

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
3. MEMBER STATE SCREENING MECHANISMS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Regulation requires Member States to notify the Commission and other Member States
of FDI transactions undergoing screening in their jurisdiction.

As of 2024, the following major EU economies have national FDI screening mechanisms:
- GERMANY: Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) + Foreign Trade and Payments Ordinance
  (AWV). Sectors: critical infrastructure, media, defense, health. Thresholds: 10%-25%
  depending on sector. Mandatory for certain defense/critical infrastructure sectors.
- FRANCE: Decree 2019-1590. Broad scope — national defense, public security, public
  policy. Mandatory filing for controlling investments in sensitive sectors.
- NETHERLANDS: National Security Screening Act (Vifo Act, 2023). Critical infrastructure,
  technology. Mandatory filing for acquisitions in covered sectors.
- ITALY: "Golden Powers" Decree. Broad sectors: defense, telecom, energy, transport,
  finance. Mandatory notification. Government may impose conditions or veto.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
4. EUROPEAN COMMISSION ROLE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Commission may issue opinions on FDI transactions that:
(a) Are likely to affect security or public order in more than one Member State; or
(b) Could affect EU projects or programs of Union interest (TEN-E, TEN-T, Horizon Europe,
    Galileo, EGNOS, Copernicus, Connecting Europe Facility, etc.)

Commission opinions are non-binding but carry significant weight. Member State where
screening occurs must take the Commission's opinion into utmost account.

EXTRATERRITORIAL EFFECT:
If FDI is not subject to screening in any Member State, the Commission itself may issue
opinions if the investment affects Union security/public order. This creates a "backstop"
Commission screening role even where no Member State has jurisdiction.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
5. STRENGTHENED REGULATION — 2024 PROPOSAL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

In January 2024, the European Commission proposed a strengthened FDI Regulation to:
1. Require ALL Member States to have national screening mechanisms (currently optional)
2. Expand sectoral scope to align with EU critical raw materials, chips, and AI strategies
3. Introduce mandatory notification for certain cross-border transactions affecting EU
4. Reduce review timelines
5. Strengthen anti-circumvention rules (shell companies, structured investments)

This strengthened regulation is expected to significantly increase the number of
transactions subject to EU FDI review.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
6. CROSS-BORDER INVESTMENT IMPLICATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

FOR NON-EU INVESTORS ENTERING EU:
- Technology sector investments: Must assess Germany, France, Netherlands, Italy
  screening requirements at minimum
- Energy/infrastructure: Likely triggers multiple Member State reviews
- Media: France and Germany have specific media ownership restrictions

CHINESE INVESTMENT IN EU:
Chinese investments in EU critical sectors face increasing scrutiny. Several German
acquisitions of semiconductor and critical technology companies by Chinese buyers
have been blocked or unwound (e.g., Aixtron attempted acquisition — blocked by German
government following US pressure, 2016; Siltronic — blocked 2022).

STRUCTURING THROUGH EU MEMBER STATES:
Routing Chinese investment through an EU-based holding company does NOT avoid
FDI screening if the ultimate controlling party is non-EU. The look-through to
beneficial ownership is standard in EU Member State screening processes.

TIMING:
FDI screenings in EU Member States can take 90-150 days (or longer for complex deals).
Pre-signing engagement with competent authorities (where pre-notification is possible)
is strongly recommended for investments in covered sectors.
