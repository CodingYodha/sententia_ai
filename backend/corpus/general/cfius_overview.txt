COMMITTEE ON FOREIGN INVESTMENT IN THE UNITED STATES (CFIUS)
FIRRMA Overview and Investment Screening Framework
Source: U.S. Department of Treasury, CFIUS
Foreign Investment Risk Review Modernization Act of 2018 (FIRRMA) | 31 C.F.R. Part 800 and Part 802

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
1. OVERVIEW AND STATUTORY BASIS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

CFIUS is an interagency committee of the U.S. Government authorized to review certain
transactions involving foreign investment in the United States to determine the effect
of such transactions on the national security of the United States.

STATUTORY BASIS:
- Section 721 of the Defense Production Act of 1950 (as amended by FIRRMA 2018)
- Implemented by regulations at 31 C.F.R. Part 800 (covered transactions) and
  31 C.F.R. Part 802 (real estate transactions)
- Additional guidance in CFIUS annual reports to Congress

COMMITTEE MEMBERS:
Treasury (Chair), State, Defense, DOJ, Commerce, DHS, USTR, OMB, OSTP, NSA, DNI, DOE
+ agency heads invited as appropriate (e.g., DOT for transportation transactions)

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
2. COVERED TRANSACTIONS — WHAT CFIUS REVIEWS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

COVERED CONTROL TRANSACTIONS (Part 800):
Any transaction (including mergers, acquisitions, certain investments) that could result
in foreign control of any U.S. business, regardless of sector.

"Control" means the power, direct or indirect, whether or not exercised, to determine,
direct, or decide important matters affecting an entity.

COVERED NON-CONTROLLING INVESTMENTS (Part 800 — TID U.S. Businesses):
Even non-controlling foreign investments in certain "TID U.S. businesses" are covered:
- Technology: U.S. businesses involved in critical technology (defined by export control laws —
  EAR, ITAR, nuclear-related, select agents)
- Infrastructure: U.S. businesses that own or operate critical infrastructure
  (financial market infrastructure, power grid, water systems, telecom, ports, etc.)
- Data: U.S. businesses that collect or maintain sensitive personal data of U.S. persons
  (financial data, health data, geolocation data, biometric data, etc.)

For TID investments, "covered" even if foreign investor only gets:
- Access to material non-public technical information
- Membership on board of directors or board observer rights
- Involvement in substantive decision-making regarding TID aspects

COVERED REAL ESTATE TRANSACTIONS (Part 802):
Foreign purchase, lease, or concession of certain real estate proximate to military
installations, U.S. government facilities, or certain airports/ports.

3. MANDATORY VS. VOLUNTARY FILINGS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

MANDATORY DECLARATIONS (cannot be waived — short form, 30-day review):
Required for transactions where:
(a) Foreign government holds ≥ 25% economic interest in the foreign investor AND
    the transaction involves a TID U.S. business; OR
(b) Transaction involves a U.S. critical technology business AND foreign investor is from
    certain countries (updated annually by USTR) — "excepted foreign states" currently
    includes Australia, Canada, UK, New Zealand (Five Eyes partners)

VOLUNTARY NOTICE (full notice, 45-day review + 45-day investigation if warranted):
For covered transactions not subject to mandatory declaration, parties may voluntarily
file a notice with CFIUS. Voluntary filing provides "safe harbor" — if CFIUS clears
the transaction, it cannot be reopened unless information was materially false/omitted.

Parties who do NOT file voluntarily risk:
- Post-closing CFIUS review at any time (no statute of limitations for national security)
- Forced divestiture order by President of the United States
- Civil monetary penalties up to $250,000 per violation (per day) or transaction value

4. CHINESE INVESTMENT — HEIGHTENED SCRUTINY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PRC-NEXUS TRANSACTIONS:
Chinese investment in U.S. businesses receives heightened CFIUS scrutiny across all
sectors, including those not traditionally considered sensitive. CFIUS has increased
enforcement actions, mandatory filing requirements, and mitigation agreement requirements
for PRC-nexus transactions.

KEY CONCERNS IDENTIFIED BY CFIUS FOR PRC INVESTORS:
- Technology transfer risk (export of U.S. technology to China)
- IP theft risk through board/observer positions
- Data access risk for PRC government
- Influence over U.S. critical infrastructure
- Supply chain compromise

STRUCTURING THROUGH THIRD COUNTRIES:
CFIUS looks through nominal investors to identify foreign government control and
beneficial ownership. Routing PRC investment through Singapore, Cayman Islands, or
other third countries does NOT avoid CFIUS review if:
- The ultimate beneficial owner or controlling party is Chinese
- A Chinese government-affiliated entity has rights or influence in the investment
- Chinese investor provides financing or has call/put rights on the investment

FIRRMA made explicit that CFIUS has jurisdiction over any transaction where the
foreign person is "controlled by" a foreign government — PRC state-owned enterprises
(SOEs) and entities with significant PRC government involvement are automatically
subject to heightened review.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
5. REVIEW PROCESS AND TIMELINES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DECLARATION REVIEW: Up to 30 days
  CFIUS may: (a) clear, (b) request full notice, (c) initiate own review

NOTICE REVIEW: 45 days (initial review)
  If CFIUS determines national security risk may exist → 45-day investigation period

INVESTIGATION: Additional 45 days
  Parties may be asked to provide mitigation measures, additional information

PRESIDENTIAL ACTION: President has 15 days after CFIUS recommendation to act
  Presidential action (rare but ultimate): block or unwind transaction

MITIGATION AGREEMENTS:
CFIUS frequently clears transactions subject to mitigation conditions:
- National Security Agreement (NSA): Ongoing commitments re: personnel, access, data
- Special Security Agreement (SSA): Enhanced controls for classified information access
- Proxy Agreements: Transfer of control to U.S. persons for national security matters

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
6. IMPACT ON CROSS-BORDER STRUCTURING
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

For fund investments into U.S. companies:
- PE/VC funds with Chinese LPs investing ≥ 25% of fund capital → CFIUS mandatory
  filing required for TID U.S. businesses
- Fund GPs must assess LP nationality in structuring CFIUS compliance

For direct investment into U.S. by non-U.S. acquiror:
- Even majority acquisitions by non-sensitive country investors may trigger CFIUS
  if U.S. target has TID characteristics
- Pre-signing CFIUS risk assessment is standard practice for M&A

EXCEPTED INVESTOR STATUS:
Investors from Australia, Canada, UK, New Zealand may qualify as "excepted investors"
for certain non-controlling TID investments, with reduced CFIUS obligations.
This status is earned/certified, not automatic — requires meeting criteria.
