TREATY SHOPPING, GENERAL ANTI-AVOIDANCE RULES (GAAR), AND BEPS ACTION 6
Comprehensive Overview for Cross-Border Investment Structuring
Source: OECD BEPS Action Plan; Indian Income Tax Act (Chapter X-A); MLI

1. TREATY SHOPPING — DEFINITION AND MECHANICS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DEFINITION:
Treaty shopping refers to the practice of a person (not resident in either contracting
state of a tax treaty) attempting to obtain benefits of that treaty by interposing an
entity that IS resident in one of the contracting states.

CLASSIC STRUCTURE (Direct Conduit):
  Actual Investor (Country C, no treaty with Country B)
    ↓
  Conduit Entity (Country A, has favorable DTAA with Country B)
    ↓
  Target Investment (Country B — source of income)
  
The conduit entity in Country A exists primarily to access DTAA benefits between
Countries A and B, which the actual investor in Country C could not directly access.

TREATY SHOPPING MOTIVATIONS:
- Capital gains exemptions (e.g., pre-2017 Singapore-India or Mauritius-India treaties)
- Reduced withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties
- Enhanced investment protection under bilateral investment treaties (BITs)
- Dispute resolution access through ISDS clauses

2. BEPS ACTION 6 — PREVENTING TREATY ABUSE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The OECD BEPS (Base Erosion and Profit Shifting) project identified treaty abuse
(including treaty shopping) as one of the most critical sources of BEPS. BEPS Action 6
introduced minimum standards and recommendations to prevent treaty shopping.

BEPS ACTION 6 MINIMUM STANDARD:
Countries must include in their tax treaties one of:
(a) Combined approach: Limitation on Benefits (LoB) article + Principal Purpose Test (PPT)
(b) PPT alone
(c) LoB article alone (if supplemented by domestic anti-avoidance)

MULTILATERAL INSTRUMENT (MLI):
The BEPS Multilateral Convention to Implement Tax Treaty Related Measures to Prevent BEPS
allows countries to modify bilateral tax treaties simultaneously. Over 100 jurisdictions
have signed. India has ratified the MLI, modifying its covered agreements.

3. PRINCIPAL PURPOSE TEST (PPT) — DETAILED ANALYSIS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PPT PROVISION (MLI Article 7 / OECD MTC Article 29(9)):
"Notwithstanding the other provisions of this Convention, a benefit under this Convention
shall not be granted in respect of an item of income or capital if it is reasonable to
conclude, having regard to all relevant facts and circumstances, that obtaining that
benefit was one of the principal purposes of any arrangement or transaction that
resulted directly or indirectly in that benefit, unless it is established that granting
that benefit in these circumstances would be in accordance with the object and purpose
of the relevant provisions of this Convention."

KEY ELEMENTS:
1. "REASONABLE TO CONCLUDE": An objective standard. Tax authority must show it is
   reasonable — not that treaty shopping was the ONLY purpose, just ONE of the PRINCIPAL purposes.
   
2. "ONE OF THE PRINCIPAL PURPOSES": Even if a structure has legitimate business purposes,
   if treaty benefit was also a principal purpose, PPT may apply. Not de minimis.
   
3. BURDEN SHIFTS: Once tax authority shows a principal purpose of obtaining benefit,
   taxpayer must show that granting the benefit accords with the "object and purpose" of
   the treaty provision.

4. RELEVANT FACTORS (OECD Commentary on Article 29):
   - When the arrangement was set up relative to when income arose
   - Whether the arrangement produces significant treaty benefits for one party
   - Whether the arrangement results in the income not being taxed in the intermediary's country
   - Whether the conduit entity carries on genuine business activities
   - Substance and economic reality of the arrangement

PPT APPLICATION EXAMPLES (from OECD Commentary):

EXAMPLE A — PPT APPLIES:
Company R in Country R holds shares in Company S in Country S. Treaty between R and S
exempts source-state taxation of capital gains. Company R was established immediately
before a planned sale of Company S shares by its parent in Country T (which has no treaty
with Country S). The principal purpose of interposing Company R was to access the treaty
exemption. PPT denies the exemption.

EXAMPLE B — PPT DOES NOT APPLY:
Company R in Country R has operated genuine business for 10 years. It holds shares in
Country S subsidiary as part of diversified global operations. Shares were acquired in
the ordinary course of business. Capital gains treaty exemption applies upon sale.
PPT does not apply — obtaining treaty benefit was not a principal purpose; it was
incidental to genuine business operations.

4. LIMITATION ON BENEFITS (LoB) — US MODEL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

LOB ARTICLE (typically found in US bilateral treaties):
A more mechanical test than PPT. Treaty benefits are limited to persons who satisfy
one of the "qualifying person" tests:

QUALIFYING PERSON TESTS:
- Individual resident: Automatically qualifies
- Publicly listed company: Shares regularly traded on recognized exchange in one or both
  Contracting States
- Subsidiary: If shares owned ≥ 50% by qualifying persons, and meets "erosion test"
  (< 50% of income used to make deductible payments to non-treaty-country persons)
- Active business test: Person engaged in active conduct of trade or business in residence
  state, and income is connected/incidental to that business
- Derivative benefits test: Benefits would have been available if income had gone directly
  to persons in third states who are residents of treaty states and themselves qualifying

LoB APPROACH vs PPT APPROACH:
- LoB: More mechanical/predictable; provides certainty for genuine business entities
- PPT: More flexible/subjective; broader anti-abuse coverage; lower threshold to apply
- Combination: Many post-2017 treaties use PPT + simplified LoB

5. INDIA GAAR — CHAPTER X-A INCOME TAX ACT 1961
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

EFFECTIVE DATE: April 1, 2017 (for arrangements entered into from FY 2017-18 onwards)
STATUTORY BASIS: Sections 95-102 of Income Tax Act 1961 (Chapter X-A)

SECTION 96 — IMPERMISSIBLE AVOIDANCE ARRANGEMENT (IAA):
An arrangement is an IAA if its MAIN PURPOSE is to obtain a tax benefit AND:
(a) It creates rights/obligations not normally found in arm's length arrangements
(b) It results in misuse or abuse of provisions of the Act
(c) It lacks commercial substance
(d) It is entered into by means not ordinarily employed for bona fide purposes

WHAT CONSTITUTES LACK OF COMMERCIAL SUBSTANCE (Section 97):
- Round-trip financing (funds going out and returning without genuine economic substance)
- Accommodating party: Any party acting as a mere conduit with no real economic role
- Elements offsetting each other
- Presence of equity/debt instruments that are structured primarily to achieve tax benefit
- Location of asset or transaction without substantial commercial purpose

SECTION 96(2) — PRESUMPTION:
If a business purpose of an arrangement is not evident to the Income Tax Authority,
the main purpose shall be PRESUMED to be tax benefit unless the taxpayer shows otherwise.

CONSEQUENCE OF IAA DESIGNATION:
Income tax authority may:
- Disregard the arrangement in whole or in part
- Reallocate income/expense between parties to the arrangement
- Re-characterize the nature of income or expense
- Disregard the corporate form of an entity
- Treat two or more entities as one for tax purposes

GAAR + TREATY INTERACTION:
GAAR overrides bilateral DTAA benefits where an arrangement is determined to be an IAA.
The Supreme Court of India confirmed GAAR's overriding effect on treaty benefits in
subsequent interpretations. However, GAAR is not applied lightly — tax authority must
follow prescribed procedural safeguards (Approving Panel, etc.).

GAAR SAFE HARBOR — RULE 10U:
GAAR does NOT apply if:
- Arrangement is between non-residents and concerns income not taxable in India
- Arrangement qualifies for exemption under Section 10 of ITA (with limitations)
- Tax benefit in a year is less than INR 3 crore (approximately USD 360,000) — de minimis

6. SUBSTANCE REQUIREMENTS — PRACTICAL GUIDANCE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

To withstand PPT / GAAR / anti-avoidance challenges, Singapore/Mauritius/Netherlands/UAE
intermediate entities should have genuine economic substance:

MINIMUM SUBSTANCE CHECKLIST:
☐ Registered office with genuine operations (not just a registered agent address)
☐ Local directors with relevant expertise who make genuine decisions
☐ Employees with substantive role (not just administrative staff)
☐ Board meetings conducted in jurisdiction of incorporation
☐ Local bank accounts with genuine cash flows
☐ Local expenses consistent with business scale
☐ Independent decision-making authority (not subject to routine instructions from parent)
☐ Tax registration and actual tax payments in jurisdiction
☐ No contractual obligation to pass through 100% of income

SINGAPORE SPECIFIC:
Singapore's territorial tax system means that Singapore holding companies typically do
not pay Singapore tax on foreign-sourced income (Section 13(8) dividend exemption).
This reduces the "tax in hands of conduit" argument. Substance in Singapore remains
important for PPT purposes.

HIGH RISK SCENARIOS (PPT/GAAR likely to apply):
✗ SPV incorporated immediately before a planned transaction
✗ SPV with no employees, no real office, no decision-making authority
✗ SPV whose sole asset is the investment into target country
✗ SPV that passes through 100% of income/gains with no retention or business purpose
✗ Structures where treaty benefit was explicitly referenced in pre-transaction documents
✗ Structures driven primarily by a tax advisor's optimization memo

LOW RISK SCENARIOS:
✓ Entity established well before transaction with genuine business history
✓ Multiple investment activities (not just one-country concentration)
✓ Local employees with genuine investment decision-making authority
✓ Retained earnings, operating costs, genuine business activities in intermediate country
✓ Investment held for long-term (not structured for quick flip)
✓ Commercial rationale documented clearly and independently supported
