## 1. Overview of Transaction

The transaction is structured to enable Meridian Grace Foundation ("MGF") and Atlas Education Partners LLC ("AEP") to jointly support the development of Ananta International School by investing through two separate, for-profit Indian vehicles rather than into the school itself: Meridian Campus Infrastructure Private Limited ("PropCo"), which will own the land and school building, and Ananta Educare Private Limited ("OpCo"), a for-profit Educational Services Company that provides paid support services to the school.

Under this model:

- MGF establishes and capitalizes PropCo, an Indian Wholly Owned Subsidiary (WOS), to acquire land and construct and hold the school campus.
- AEP invests directly into OpCo to fund school marketing, admissions, curriculum support, and financial oversight services.
- Neither MGF nor AEP invests directly in the entity that legally runs the School itself.

Indian law requires a K-12 school to be run by a registered non-profit society, trust, or Section 8 company on a "no-profit-no-loss" basis, and prohibits it from distributing profit to any investor. This follows from the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009, the CBSE Affiliation Bye-Laws, and a settled line of Supreme Court authority running through *T.M.A. Pai Foundation v. State of Karnataka* (2002), *Islamic Academy of Education v. State of Karnataka* (2003), *P.A. Inamdar v. State of Maharashtra* (2005), and most recently *Modern Dental College & Research Centre v. State of Madhya Pradesh* (2016) 7 SCC 353, which held that education is a "noble occupation" that "would not permit commercialisation or profiteering" and cannot be treated as a "business." Ananta Education Trust ("AET"), a registered public charitable trust, will hold the CBSE affiliation and formally operate the School.

The structure creates a dual-vehicle, dual-contract model where:

1. PropCo, capitalized by MGF, acquires and constructs the campus, then leases it to AET under a registered long-term Lease Deed and earns rental income. Earning rent on a lease, without transferring the property, is expressly carved out of the prohibited "real estate business" definition under the FEMA (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019 (Schedule I, as substituted 12 April 2022), which is what makes this a permitted, FDI-eligible activity.
2. OpCo, capitalized by AEP, contracts with AET under a Management and Consultancy Agreement to provide school marketing, admissions, curriculum, and financial-oversight services, and earns consultancy fee income.
3. AET, the non-profit Trust, is the sole entity that legally owns and operates Ananta International School, receives no FDI, issues no shares, and contracts separately with PropCo and OpCo as an arm's-length counterparty for each.

This is the "PropCo-OpCo" (sometimes called the "three-pronged") model documented in Indian K-12 and higher-education private equity and FDI practice: a for-profit real-estate company, a for-profit services company, and a non-profit school operator, each performing a distinct role so that foreign capital can earn a commercial return even though the school itself cannot distribute profit.

## 3. Overall Structure of the Project

- Ananta Education Trust ("AET") will own and operate Ananta International School in Pune, Maharashtra on a no-profit-no-loss basis.
- MGF and AEP would like to fund the construction and operation of the School, but neither can invest directly in AET because it is a non-profit trust: foreign equity into a trust is not an FDI-compliant instrument under the FEMA NDI Rules, and any foreign funds reaching a trust of this kind would instead be treated as "foreign contribution" under the FCRA, 2011, requiring separate registration and consent.
- The structure therefore routes MGF's capital through PropCo, which owns the real estate and earns lease rent, and AEP's capital through OpCo, which earns consultancy fees, with AET as the sole contractual counterparty of both.
- The following sections cover the structure to achieve this objective to the extent permitted under Indian law.

## 4. Step 1 | Investment by AEP in Ananta Educare Private Limited (OpCo)

### Structure & Observations

Because OpCo will be engaged in providing educational support services, rather than in operating the School itself or in real estate, 100% FDI is permitted under the automatic route. AEP may invest through any FDI-compliant instrument: equity shares, Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS), Compulsorily Convertible Debentures (CCDs), partly paid shares, or share warrants. Equity shares are the simplest for transaction execution and mirror how a straightforward services company is usually capitalized.

### Key Points to Finalize

1. How much will AEP invest in OpCo, and at what post-money ownership percentage?
2. What shareholder rights will AEP require: board seats, reserved-matter/veto rights, information rights?
3. OpCo will need a registered valuer's FMV valuation before issuing shares to AEP, followed by the private placement process (board approval, special resolution, allotment).
4. OpCo must report the FDI to its AD Bank on Form FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment.
5. AEP should review OpCo's Management & Consultancy Agreement with AET (Step 4) as OpCo's primary revenue source, since OpCo has no direct claim on School fee income.

Ananta Educare Private Limited ("OpCo") is an Indian private company limited by shares, whose main business object is to provide educational management, marketing, admissions, curriculum, and financial-supervision support services to schools, including Ananta International School.

Atlas Education Partners LLC ("AEP") proposes to fund OpCo through investment in fresh equity shares. This is an FDI transaction regulated under the FEMA NDI Rules; the per-share price must be at least the FMV determined by a

registered valuer, and the investment must move through normal banking channels into OpCo's account with its AD Bank.

## 5. Step 2 | Incorporation of PropCo by MGF ---

### *Structure & Observations*

To incorporate PropCo, MGF will need to nominate at least 2 directors, of whom at least 1 must be an Indian resident under Section 149(3) of the Companies Act, 2013 (a director who has stayed in India for a total of at least 182 days in the financial year). PropCo will also need at least 2 shareholders, since Indian law requires a private company to have a minimum of 2 members; one can be a nominee shareholder holding a small number of shares, with MGF as the beneficial owner of the balance.

### *Key Points to Finalize*

1. PropCo's stated business object should be the acquisition, development, and leasing of real estate for educational use. This keeps PropCo within the FEMA carve-out for lease/rental income rather than the prohibited "real estate business" (defined as dealing in land and immovable property with a view to earning profit from transfer, and expressly excluding earning of rent or income on lease of property not amounting to transfer, and expressly including development of educational institutions as a permitted activity).
2. PropCo's financial assets should not exceed 50% of its total assets, and its income from financial assets should not exceed 50% of its gross income, to avoid classification as an NBFC requiring separate RBI registration.
3. Initial share capital can be nominal (for example, INR 100,000 divided into 10,000 equity shares of INR 10 each), consistent with standard WOS incorporation practice; the larger construction-capital tranche follows in Step 3.

MGF will incorporate Meridian Campus Infrastructure Private Limited ("PropCo") as an Indian Wholly Owned Subsidiary, a private company limited by shares.

Incorporation must comply with the Companies Act, 2013 and the FEMA NDI Rules, and will require KYC documents notarized and apostilled in the US. PAN and TAN are issued at incorporation via the SPICE+ portal; GST registration and any other licenses follow once PropCo's specific construction and leasing activity is finalized.

## 6. Step 3 | Investment by MGF in PropCo for Campus Construction, and Lease to AET ---

### *Structure & Observations*

PropCo will need a larger capital tranche to acquire land and construct the school campus than its initial incorporation capital. MGF will fund this second tranche through CCPS, giving MGF a preferential dividend right and board/veto rights ahead of ordinary equity, and preserving "as-if-converted" voting under the shareholders' agreement (SHA) before conversion.

### *Key Points to Finalize*

1. How much will MGF invest in PropCo for land acquisition and construction, and over what drawdown schedule?
2. What shareholder rights will MGF require in PropCo: board seats, veto matters over disposal of the underlying property, "as-if-converted" voting on the CCPS?
3. PropCo will need an FMV valuation before issuing CCPS to MGF, followed by the private placement process, and must report the investment on Form FC-GPR within 30 days.
4. The Lease Deed between PropCo and AET should be registered, negotiated on arm's-length terms benchmarked against an independent rent valuation, and structured with disciplined, construction-linked drawdowns (for example, through an escrow mechanism) so that MGF's funds are visibly applied to campus construction.

5. PropCo's rental income from AET, after tax, becomes the source of dividends PropCo can pay MGF (see Section 8). MGF will invest in PropCo by acquiring CCPS issued by PropCo, preceded by an FMV valuation so the per-share price is at least the price determined by the registered valuer.

MGF will execute an SHA with PropCo covering board seats, veto matters (including any disposal of the underlying land or building), exit mechanics, and dividend rights.

PropCo will execute a registered long-term Lease Deed with AET for the campus, at an independently valued market rent, structured so that lease payments, rather than a transfer of the property, are the return mechanism. This is consistent with the FEMA carve-out for rent and lease income described in Section 2.

## 7. Step 4 | OpCo's Management & Consultancy Agreement with AET

### Structure & Observations

Separately from Step 1's equity investment, OpCo formalizes its revenue-generating relationship with AET through a Management and Consultancy Agreement covering governance support, curriculum design, admissions and marketing, teacher training, and financial supervision. Fees under this agreement are OpCo's primary income and, in turn, AEP's primary repatriation channel via OpCo dividends.

### Key Points to Finalize

1. Consultancy fees should be benchmarked to arm's-length, comparable market rates and supported by transfer-pricing documentation, since OpCo, PropCo, and AET are related through common sponsorship even though none holds equity in another.
2. The Agreement should require prior board approval at OpCo (and, if applicable under AET's trust deed, at AET), since it may be treated as a related-party arrangement.
3. Consultancy fees are subject to 18% GST.
4. Because AET's income derives entirely from School fees, fee-payment milestones under the Agreement should be tied to AET's fee-collection cycle, to avoid straining AET's no-profit-no-loss position.

## 8. Choice of Instruments: What Should Be Invested, and in What Form

### For PropCo

- Initial incorporation: ordinary equity shares at nominal value, consistent with standard WOS setup.
- Capex/construction tranche: CCPS. CCPS are FDI-compliant "equity instruments" under the FEMA NDI Rules, can carry a preferential dividend right ahead of ordinary equity, and, through "as-if-converted" voting arrangements in the SHA, give MGF governance rights before conversion. Because PropCo's role is to hold and lease a long-lived, income-producing asset, CCPS let MGF combine a structured, priority return with an eventual full-equity conversion, exit, or buy-back, while keeping PropCo's balance sheet on an equity, rather than debt, footing. That equity footing also helps keep PropCo clear of any accidental NBFC characterization.
- Not recommended: optionally convertible or redeemable preference shares or debentures are treated as debt (External Commercial Borrowing) under the FEMA NDI Rules, not as FDI-compliant equity, and do not fit this structure.

### For OpCo

- Equity shares are recommended for AEP's investment, mirroring the simplicity of a straightforward services-company capitalization, without the staged capex drawdown that PropCo requires. Equity shares are also administratively simpler for structuring exit and control rights around, without an embedded conversion mechanic.
- If AEP's investment were later staged (for example, an initial tranche plus a growth tranche tied to enrollment milestones), CCPS could be introduced for the second tranche to give AEP a preferential return and board rights ahead of ordinary equity, consistent with how MGF's second PropCo tranche is structured.

Both PropCo's and OpCo's allotments require an FMV valuation from a SEBI-registered merchant banker or a practicing Chartered Accountant acting as a registered valuer, and both trigger Form FC-GPR reporting to the RBI FIRMS portal within 30 days of allotment, verified by the respective company's AD Bank.

## 9. How PropCo Repatriates Profits to Its Shareholders

1. AET pays PropCo lease rent under the registered Lease Deed. Because both PropCo and AET are Indian residents, this is a purely domestic payment: AET deducts TDS at 10% under Section 194-I of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (on rent net of GST), and the rent attracts 18% GST.
2. PropCo's rental income, net of operating costs, financing costs, and Indian corporate tax, becomes distributable profit.
3. PropCo declares dividends to MGF, its shareholder. Under Article 10(2) of the India-US Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement, source-country withholding on dividends is capped at 15% where the US corporate beneficial owner holds at least 10% of PropCo's voting stock, as MGF (holding 100%) would. This is materially lower than the domestic withholding rate that would otherwise apply to a non-resident shareholder. MGF should obtain a Tax Residency Certificate and the applicable self-declaration form to claim this treaty rate.
4. If MGF and AEP later consolidate through the Alternative Structure's Delaware JV LLC (Section 10), PropCo's dividends flow to the JV LLC first, which then allocates returns between MGF and AEP under their Operating Agreement.

5. On exit, MGF could realize value through a share buy-back or sale of its PropCo shares, subject to a fresh valuation and FEMA pricing guidelines. FEMA does not permit assured or guaranteed-return exit structures priced above fair market value, so any put option or exit mechanic in the SHA should be drafted accordingly.

## 10. Alternative Structure: Single Consolidated JV LLC Channel ---

### Core Idea

- MGF and AEP consolidate their participation through a single US entity, the Meridian-Atlas Education JV LLC (Delaware).
- The JV LLC owns 100% of PropCo and 100% of OpCo, becoming the sole FDI investor into each.
- A unified channel replaces the two-pathway model, reducing the number of valuations, SHAs, and FC-GPR filings, and simplifying AET's counterparty relationships. AET still contracts separately with PropCo for the lease and OpCo for consultancy, but both entities now share a single ultimate shareholder.

### How the Money Flows

- MGF and AEP invest into the JV LLC in the US, per an agreed capital split set out in the JV LLC's Operating Agreement.
- The JV LLC capitalizes PropCo (equity plus CCPS for the construction tranche) and OpCo (equity) through two well-documented FDI inflows.
- PropCo leases the campus to AET; OpCo provides consultancy services to AET, exactly as in the primary structure.
- A single set of governance documents applies per Indian entity, and the JV LLC's Operating Agreement becomes the single place where MGF and AEP's economic split is recorded, rather than splitting it across two separate Indian shareholding registers.

### How Control Is Exercised

- The JV LLC holds all investor rights under separate SHAs with PropCo and OpCo, including board representation, veto rights, and information and financial oversight in each.
- AET contracts with PropCo and OpCo exactly as before; the JV LLC does not contract with AET directly, preserving AET's independence as the non-profit school operator.

### How Returns Flow Back

- Lease rent: AET to PropCo, as before.
- Consultancy fees: AET to OpCo, as before.
- Dividends: PropCo and OpCo each distribute to the JV LLC once profitable, at the 15% India-US DTAA dividend rate (Article 10(2)).
- The JV LLC allocates net returns to MGF and AEP according to their agreed split in the Operating Agreement.

## 12. Key Regulatory Notes & Risk Flags

1. FCRA insulation rationale. MGF is affiliated with the Sunrise Path Alliance (SPA), a global faith-based network. If MGF funded AET (the Trust) directly, this could raise scrutiny under the FCRA, 2011 given AET's informal proximity to SPA's local historical ties, and any foreign funds reaching AET would in any event be treated as "foreign contribution," requiring separate FCRA registration and consent under Sections 5(1) and 5(4) and related provisions. The Supreme Court confirmed these provisions remain valid law in *Indian Social Action Forum v. Union of India* (2020), while reading down the associated Rules for organizations working on social and economic betterment. Routing MGF's capital through PropCo, a for-profit FDI vehicle regulated by FEMA rather than FCRA, is the reason this structure is used.

2. Arm's-length pricing is essential. Both the Lease Deed rent and the Consultancy Agreement fees must be independently valued and benchmarked, since regulators and courts distinguish legitimate structuring from a "colorable device" lacking commercial substance (*McDowell & Co. v. CTO* (1985); *Union of India v. Azadi Bachao Andolan* (2003)). The General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Chapter X-A, Income Tax Act, 1961) empowers the tax authority to disregard an arrangement that lacks commercial substance.

3. CBSE non-proprietary character. AET's governing body must remain a non-proprietary trust not controlled by a single individual or family, per the CBSE Affiliation Bye-Laws. PropCo's and OpCo's contractual rights should stop short of any arrangement that gives them effective control over AET's governance.

4. Reasonable surplus, not profiteering. AET may retain a surplus for reinvestment in the School (the Supreme Court in *Islamic Academy of Education v. State of Karnataka* (2003) referenced a range ordinarily between 6% and 15%), but may not distribute profit. The combined burden of PropCo's rent and OpCo's fees on AET's fee income should be modeled against this range to avoid the arrangement being read as indirect profiteering.

5. NBFC threshold monitoring. PropCo's financial assets and financial income should each stay under 50% of its respective totals, to avoid inadvertent NBFC classification under the RBI's principal-business test.

6. No assured returns under FEMA. Any put option, buy-back, or exit-pricing mechanism in either SHA must be benchmarked to fair market value at the time of exit, not a pre-agreed guaranteed return.

7. Construction-development lock-in. The standard 3-year lock-in on FDI in construction-development projects does not apply where the project is an educational institution, but PropCo's property should not be transferred, as distinct from leased, during any lock-in period that might otherwise apply to a mixed-use development.
